Navigating the Geopolitical Stack

1. Beyond the "Cloud Region"

Selecting "AWS Frankfurt" is no longer enough. Sovereignty in 2026 requires Data Decoupling—separating the processing layer from the storage layer. Your agents can run globally, but PII must remain anchored in its territory of origin. This is the difference between a global product and a local liability.

2. The Rise of "National Clouds"

We are seeing the emergence of state-backed or state-mandated cloud providers. Integrating these into your stack without creating an unmanageable "spaghetti architecture" is the primary challenge for the modern CTO. The solution is a Middleware Layer that abstracts the vendor while enforcing the residency policy.

3. Data as a Diplomatic Asset

Trust is the new currency. Organizations that can prove deterministic data sovereignty to their customers win the high-LTV contracts. In the B2B space, "Where is my data?" is the first question asked in the RFP. If you don't have a punchy, data-backed answer, you've already lost the deal.

The Sunder Perspective: Stop treating data residency as a legal hurdle. Treat it as a sales advantage. When you can tell a European client that their data never touches a US server, you've removed the biggest friction point in the B2B sales cycle.
