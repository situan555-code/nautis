Example Experiences

Furniture first, and one case that is not furniture.

These cards are examples of the scenes Sunder builds. They are not products for sale and they do not have their own URLs.

Modular sectional

Fabric, chaise side, and how it sits in a room.

The visitor changes fabric, which end the chaise is on, and how many seats. The scene shows the piece at room scale.

The named options are fabric, chaise side, seat count, and leg finish.

Dining table

Top, base, and an extension leaf.

The visitor changes the wood, the base, and whether the leaf is in, with chairs at the scale of the top.

The named options are top material, base, extension, and chair count.

Upholstered bed

Headboard height and textile.

The visitor changes the headboard profile, the cloth, and the bed size.

The named options are headboard, textile, and size.

Sideboard

Case finish and hardware.

The visitor changes the case finish, the door style, and the pull.

The named options are finish, doors, and hardware.

Outdoor lounge

Frame, sling, and cushion.

The visitor changes the frame color, the sling, and the cushion on a porch-scale piece.

The named options are frame, sling, and cushion.

Beyond furniture

The same kind of scene for a product that is not a sofa.

The visitor turns, scales, and finishes one machined object in the browser. The page is furniture-led. It is not furniture-only. This object does not get its own URL.

The named options are material, finish, and scale.

Crawlable Summary

The choices stay in the HTML.

Option names belong in the page, not only in the scene: fabric, chaise side, seat count, leg finish, top material, base, extension, chair count, headboard, textile, size, case finish, doors, hardware, frame, sling, cushion, material, and scale.

A client-only configurator with no crawlable summary is the failure mode the home furnishings AI visibility audit describes. This page should not be that failure mode.
